title: 'Permanent residence card based on Polish descent: from two refusals to a positive result'
lead: The applicant obtained permanent residence based on Polish descent after two refusals.
situation: |-
  A Ukrainian citizen came to us after a long wait and unsuccessful previous handling of his case by another specialist. His first application was refused because the previous representative had not submitted the original translations of the documents. After that, the applicant never received proper information about the progress of his case, and all the original family documents remained at the Voivodeship Office.

  After coming to us, the applicant reapplied but was refused again. Among other things, the Voivode pointed out that in his earlier temporary residence application the applicant had stated Ukrainian nationality, and concluded that the lack of direct documentary proof of Polish descent in the relevant line of ancestry did not justify granting a permanent residence permit.

  The situation was complicated by the fact that the applicant's and his father's documents listed the parents as Ukrainian, while archival documents of the previous generation confirmed Polish nationality. A full assessment of the case required reconstructing its history and researching archival materials.
done: |-
  - We reconstructed the history of the case and analysed the available materials and archival documents.
  - We researched documents of several generations of the family to substantiate the applicant's Polish descent.
  - We prepared a thorough appeal against the refusal, arguing the case in light of the historical and political circumstances that may have affected how nationality was recorded in official documents.
  - We provided a legal assessment of the Voivode's arguments about the lack of direct documentary proof of descent.
  - We helped the applicant recover all the family documents held at the Voivodeship Office.
result: Despite the earlier setbacks and the difficult documentary situation, the applicant obtained a permanent residence permit based on Polish descent. All the family documents were also returned to him.
moral: This case shows that a refusal does not always mean there are no legal grounds for a permit. In complex cases it is important not only to collect documents, but also to reconstruct the history of earlier proceedings, research archival materials and argue the legal position consistently.
